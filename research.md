## Relevant Industry Standards or Protocols

- **ISO 45001:2018** — International standard for occupational health and safety management systems; primary compliance framework for incident management, risk assessment, and corrective actions
- **ISO 14001:2015** — Environmental management systems standard; governs environmental aspect identification, legal compliance, and reduction programmes
- **OSHA 29 CFR 1910 / 1926** — US Occupational Safety and Health Administration regulations for general industry and construction; EHS systems must support OSHA recordkeeping (300/300A logs)
- **GHS / UN Globally Harmonised System** — Chemical classification and labelling standard; relevant for SDS management and chemical inventory modules
- **REACH / RoHS (EU)** — European chemical substance regulation; compliance tracking required in chemical management modules
- **ISO 50001** — Energy management systems standard; relevant for EHS platforms that extend into sustainability and carbon accounting
- **TCFD / GRI / CSRD** — ESG disclosure frameworks driving demand for environmental data collection within EHS platforms

## Market Research

**Market Size:** The EHS software market was valued at approximately USD 8.21 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach USD 8.9 billion in 2026 at a CAGR of roughly 8.4%, growing to USD 12.15 billion by 2030.

**Funding:** The market is led by established players — VelocityEHS, Intelex (acquired by Fortive), Cority, and Enablon (Wolters Kluwer). Newer SaaS entrants like Evotix and SafetyCulture have raised significant venture rounds. ESG reporting mandates (CSRD, SEC climate rules) are driving increased corporate investment in EHS platforms as part of broader sustainability initiatives.

**Pricing Landscape:** Entry-level EHS tools start around $23/month; mid-market platforms targeting SMBs average around $188/month. Enterprise platforms (VelocityEHS, Cority, Enablon) use custom pricing with annual contracts typically in the tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands of dollars. Intelex Essentials introduced a lower-cost SMB tier in 2025.

**Key Buyer Personas:** EHS managers and corporate safety directors at industrial, manufacturing, and construction companies; sustainability officers managing ESG disclosure; HR and compliance teams at companies with significant chemical or hazardous materials handling; operations managers in logistics and field services.

**Notable Trends:** AI capability has shifted from differentiator to baseline expectation by 2026. Predictive risk analytics — using ML to surface incident precursor patterns before events occur — is the primary competitive differentiator. Computer vision for automated hazard detection from site imagery, IoT wearable integration, and voice-enabled field reporting are all in active deployment. ESG disclosure mandates (EU CSRD) are accelerating demand for environmental data modules embedded within EHS platforms.

## AI-Native Opportunity

- Predictive incident modelling that cross-references near-miss reports, inspection findings, corrective action completion rates, and environmental sensor data to identify leading indicators of incidents before they happen
- Computer vision integration for real-time PPE compliance detection and hazard identification from site cameras, dramatically reducing reliance on manual inspection cycles
- AI-generated regulatory compliance gap analysis that monitors new regulations, interprets their applicability to specific operations, and surfaces required corrective actions automatically
- Voice-to-text incident reporting for hands-free field use, reducing friction in near-miss and incident capture and improving data completeness
- Natural language chemical safety queries — workers can ask plain-language questions about SDS data, exposure limits, and incompatibilities without navigating complex document hierarchies
